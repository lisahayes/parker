Cherry Creek Has Actually Done This Before

On August 3, 1933, at 1:20 in the morning, the Castlewood Dam south of Franktown gave way and sent a fifteen-foot wall of water tearing down Cherry Creek Canyon toward Denver. Parker's own telephone operator at the time, Nettie Driscoll, was the one who got word out to Denver police before the flood arrived, a detail that's stuck around in local history precisely because the warning came from Parker itself. Three decades later, in June 1965, Douglas County saw the largest flood in its recorded history when a surge came down Cherry Creek and killed two people on its way toward Denver. The Army Corps of Engineers built the Cherry Creek Dam in direct response, finishing it in 1950, and the Town of Parker still maintains an active floodplain management program today because several drainageways running through town remain genuinely subject to flooding after a heavy rain.

Most calls we actually take have nothing to do with the creek, a slab leak, a failed water heater, a supply line finally giving out. But that history is part of why we don't treat water intrusion here as a minor inconvenience.

The Master-Planned Slab Problem

Parker's neighborhoods, Stonegate, Stroh Ranch, Idyllwilde, Canterberry Crossing, and the early phases of Crowfoot Valley, were built between 1990 and 2015 almost entirely on concrete slab foundations with copper or PEX lines running through or beneath the concrete. When a line under one of those slabs fails, the water has nowhere obvious to go. It escapes into the soil under the foundation first and only shows up later as a warm spot on the floor or a hairline of moisture along a baseboard, often well after the leak actually started.

Extraction and Drying, Handled as One Visit

Once we've confirmed the source, a slab leak, a failed appliance, storm intrusion, extraction and structural drying happen together instead of two separate appointments days apart. Air movers and a commercial dehumidifier go in the same day, and we track moisture daily in framing, subfloor, and drywall until the readings actually confirm a safe baseline. Skipping that confirmation because the floor feels dry is the most common reason a water event here turns into a mold call a month later.

What Actually Decides How Water Gets Treated

A burst supply line is Category 1, clean water, straightforward extraction and drying. Water that's picked up contamination from an appliance or come in during a storm moves to Category 2 or 3 and needs real disinfection first. We make that call on the first visit, since it decides which materials dry in place and which come out, and it's the detail your insurance adjuster is going to ask about.

What You Actually Get as Documentation

You get a daily moisture log tied to specific locations in the house, not a single verbal all-clear at the end. That written record is what most Colorado adjusters expect when a claim involves extended drying time, and it protects you if a dispute ever comes up later about whether the work was genuinely completed to standard.

Questions

Water Damage Restoration Questions From Parker Homeowners

How do I tell if this is genuinely urgent?

Shut off every fixture and appliance, then watch your water meter for ten minutes. Any movement at all means water's escaping somewhere, and on a Parker slab that's often happening under the concrete rather than anywhere you can actually see.

Does a standard Colorado policy cover this kind of damage?

Most policies cover sudden water damage and often include access coverage for opening a slab, though the pipe repair itself usually gets billed separately. Flooding from Cherry Creek is a different category and typically needs its own flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program.

My home's in one of the newer Parker subdivisions. Does that matter?

It changes what we check first. Homes built after 2000 tend to run PEX rather than copper, which handles the area's hard water differently, so we confirm the plumbing material before assuming a cause.

Meter won't sit still, or water's already showing? Call before it spreads further under the slab.
