Flood Damage Cleanup in Parker, CO

Flood damage means water from outside your plumbing entirely, and Cherry Creek has demonstrated more than once that it can put that water into Douglas County homes.

A Creek With a Real Flood Record

Cherry Creek's flood history in this county runs back to 1933, when the Castlewood Dam south of Franktown failed at 1:20 in the morning and sent a fifteen-foot surge down the canyon toward Denver, a flood Parker's own telephone operator helped warn the city about before it arrived. The June 1965 flood, still the largest in Douglas County's recorded history, killed two people along the same creek corridor. The Army Corps of Engineers finished the Cherry Creek Dam in 1950 specifically to prevent a repeat, and the Town of Parker's own floodplain management program still tracks several drainageways through town that remain genuinely subject to flooding during heavy rain.

The Town's engineering department maintains current Cherry Creek flow data specifically because localized flooding, even without a dam failure, still shows up after a strong storm, and Parker's rapid development has in places outpaced the drainage capacity built to handle it.

Assessing Contamination Before Anything Else Happens

Floodwater typically carries silt, debris, and contamination that clean supply-line water doesn't, which changes the whole approach. We assess the water's source and category on arrival, extract with equipment rated for the actual contamination level, and evaluate porous materials for removal rather than automatically drying them in place.

Rebuild Once the Space Is Confirmed Fully Dry

Once drying is confirmed complete, rebuild follows the same material-by-material process as any water damage job, though flood-affected spaces more often need full material replacement given the contamination involved. Drying and rebuild run as one continuous project through our own team, not a gap between two separate companies.

Documentation for an Actual Flood Claim

Standard Colorado homeowners policies generally don't cover flooding from an external source like a creek; that typically requires separate coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program. We document the source, category, and a written timeline with photographs regardless, since that record determines whether a claim routes through a standard policy or needs flood coverage instead.

Standard Safety Measures on Every Flood Call

Crews working a flood cleanup wear protective gear rated for the water's contamination level for the entire job, not just the initial extraction, and we set up a decontamination zone at the exit point so debris and residue don't track through the rest of the house on the way out.

Handling Contents That Can't Be Salvaged

Flood-contaminated items that can't safely be cleaned get inventoried and photographed before disposal, since that documentation is often what an insurance claim needs for contents coverage separate from the structure itself.

How Soon Should a Flood Loss Actually Get Reported

As soon as reasonably possible. Most policies expect prompt reporting and reasonable steps to limit further damage, and waiting because you're hoping it dries out on its own can work against a claim later, since an adjuster may treat the delay as contributing to the extent of the damage.

Flood Damage Cleanup Questions From Parker Homeowners

Does my regular homeowners policy cover Cherry Creek flooding?

Usually not. Standard policies typically exclude flooding from an external source like a creek, which generally requires separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private carrier.

What's actually different about flood water versus a burst pipe?

The source and the contamination level. A burst pipe is clean water from your own plumbing. Flood water comes from outside and usually carries silt or contamination, which changes both the safety precautions we take and what can realistically be saved.

Does the Cherry Creek Dam mean Parker's actually protected now?

It substantially reduces the risk of the kind of catastrophic failure that happened in 1933, but localized flooding from heavy storms still affects specific drainageways through town, which is why the Town still maintains an active floodplain program.

What happens to belongings that got wet during a flood event?

Anything that can be safely cleaned gets treated and dried. Porous items that genuinely can't be saved get documented with photos before disposal, since that record often matters for a contents claim.
